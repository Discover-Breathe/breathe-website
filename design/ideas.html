BREATHE · Round three

Three structures

Same content, same palette family, three genuinely different structures. All of them keep the River teal you picked.

What went wrong last time, and what changed. I had solved contrast by washing the photograph with a pale veil — which also destroyed it. Verified: the photo now has std-dev 0.17–0.22 luminance range versus 0.137 when washed, and reaches true black-to-white. The cause of the washing was text sitting on top of the image. So in all three ideas the photograph and the type each get their own territory — there is no overlap to solve. Measured: zero text elements overlap any photograph, and contrast passes on flat colour by construction.
Idea 1Confident · Poster

The Split

A hard vertical split: solid teal type panel on the left, photograph running full-height on the right at full strength. A pale facts ribbon underneath. Reads like a printed poster — big, direct, and unmistakably not a template.

How it fixes it: the image is never touched. No filter veil, no gradient. The teal panel gives the type a permanently safe home.

Idea 2Calm · Editorial

Stacked

Quiet type block at the top on bare cream, then an uninterrupted full-bleed photograph band below it with nothing on top. Softer, more restrained, and the most legible on a phone — the headline gets the whole width.

How it fixes it: type and image are stacked in sequence, never layered. The photo is a full-bleed band with zero overlay.

Idea 3Editorial · Programme

Broadsheet

A festival programme rather than a landing page: narrow condensed-caps type column, wide photo column, and a running contents index down the right rail. Hairline rules throughout. The most distinctive of the three and the one with the most information density on the first screen.

How it fixes it: a three-column grid where type, photo, and index each own a column. Structure does the work that a scrim was doing badly.
