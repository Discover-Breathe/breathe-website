BREATHE · Website redesign · Direction chosen: C

Three directions

Real, responsive, single-file prototypes — same content, three different design languages. All three are static HTML with no build step and nothing but Google Fonts and the existing festival photography as external dependencies. Open each at desktop and phone widths.

Option C was chosen. It now lives at the repository root as the real site (index.html) with two changes: a daylight palette instead of night, and Schibsted Grotesk + Inter Tight instead of a serif. These prototypes are kept as a record.

Option A Warm · Editorial

Golden Hour

Deep forest green, warm cream, and amber. A high-contrast serif display face over a clean grotesque, generous whitespace, and full-bleed photography. The most premium and calm of the three — closer to a beautifully printed book than a festival flyer. Leans on the existing brand colours and makes them feel expensive.

Type: Fraunces (variable serif) · Karla

Option B Bright · Playful

Sunprint

Daylight. The brand's own blue family — the #BFD5DD to #1E2D33 ramp from your existing site — against warm paper and amber. Chunky Bricolage Grotesque, hard 3 px borders, offset shadows, tilted photo frames and a sticker. Warm, human, and a little hand-made without tipping into scrapbook. The friendliest of the three, and the only one built on the palette you already own.

Type: Bricolage Grotesque · Instrument Sans

Option C Cinematic · Atmospheric

Afterglow

Near-black, firelight ember, and an editorial serif. Photography-first with cinematic full-bleed sections and glass panels. The most emotional and evocative — it sells the feeling of being there at dusk. Strongest for a first-time visitor who doesn't yet know what BREATHE is.

Type: Instrument Serif · Inter Tight

Notes on all three

  • Responsive from 320 px to ultrawide; no horizontal overflow at any width tested.
  • WCAG AA contrast verified programmatically — small text ≥ 4.5:1, large text ≥ 3:1. Zero failures.
  • Keyboard focus visible, prefers-reduced-motion respected.
  • No JS framework, no build step — every page is one HTML file, ready for GitHub Pages.
  • Photography is pulled from the existing site so the mockups read as real, not as grey boxes.
